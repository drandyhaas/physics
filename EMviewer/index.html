Push wire resistance off zero and the field inside the plain wire stops being zero. That is Ohm's law, E = ρJ, sitting inside the metal — and the energy flow tips inward along the whole run instead of gliding past it.

How this is computed

The wire is a filament carrying an unknown surface charge. Ohm's law fixes the potential everywhere along it — flat along ideal wire, sloping across the LED, stepping up across the battery — so the charge is whatever reproduces that potential. The app solves that boundary-value problem, then takes E from Coulomb and B from Biot–Savart. Nothing here is drawn by hand; the field shapes are the solution.

The LED is modelled as a plain resistor, the wire as a thin filament, and everything is shown in the plane of the circuit, where B points purely in and out of the screen.
