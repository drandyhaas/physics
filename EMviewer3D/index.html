Slice plane

—

A flat cut through the field, for the colour map and for the readout: hovering reports the field where the cursor's line of sight crosses this plane. With z = 0 and a flat loop it is the planar field bench, seen from wherever you happen to be standing.

Rendering

—

Field lines are seeded on a lattice filling the space around the circuit, and the arrows ride on them: a line's tangent is the field direction, so an arrow on a line already points the right way. The two switch independently — arrows alone give a flow without the curves. Arrows in weak field fade rather than being cut off, so what thins out is the field thinning out.

How this is computed

The wire is a closed filament in space carrying an unknown line charge density. Ohm's law fixes the potential along it — flat along ideal wire, sloping across the LED, stepping up across the battery — so the charge is whatever reproduces that potential. The app solves that boundary-value problem, then takes E from Coulomb and B from the exact field of each finite straight filament.

The Coulomb kernel was always three-dimensional, so the charge solve is the same one the planar bench does. What is new is that B is a full vector rather than one component, which is why the loop no longer has to lie in a plane.
